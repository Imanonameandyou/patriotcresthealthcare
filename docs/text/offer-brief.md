Offer Brief
Core offer: a no-obligation health coverage options review with an independent licensed agent
Primary working audience: self-employed individuals and small-business owners in Florida

## 2. Level of Consciousness: Low / High
• Generally medium-to-high problem consciousness: prospects know premiums, deductibles, renewal increases, or loss of employer coverage are painful.
• Solution consciousness varies: some know only the ACA Marketplace or employer coverage; others have heard of private plans but do not understand underwriting, exclusions, networks, or benefit limits.
• Marketing implication: lead with the problem they already recognize (cost, uncertainty, confusing choices), then explain that different coverage categories work differently. Do not assume the prospect understands insurance terminology.
## 3. Level of Awareness
• Primary: problem-aware and solution-aware. They know they need coverage or want to review what they have, but may not know which categories of coverage are available or appropriate.
• Secondary: product-aware prospects comparing ACA Marketplace plans, off-exchange ACA-compliant plans, medically underwritten options, short-term limited-duration coverage, fixed-indemnity or supplemental products, and other arrangements.
• Less-aware prospects: people newly self-employed, leaving employer coverage, aging off a parent's plan, or facing a renewal increase. Educate first; do not begin with a product pitch.
## 4. Stage of Sophistication
• Working hypothesis: Stage 3–4 in crowded digital insurance markets. Consumers have encountered many “save money” and “better coverage” claims and may distrust broad promises.
• Differentiate through transparent explanation, independent comparison where available, needs-based discovery, and explicit discussion of trade-offs—not louder savings claims.
• This stage assessment is a strategic hypothesis, not a measured market study. Test it with live calls, lead quality, objections, and conversion data.
## 5. Big Idea
You should not have to guess what your health insurance actually covers. Before choosing or changing coverage, get a clear, personal review of the options available to you—and understand the costs, limitations, and trade-offs.
Core emotional promise: clarity and confidence in the decision. The service promises guidance and a review, not guaranteed savings, guaranteed acceptance, or a particular coverage outcome.
## 6. Metaphor
“A map before the journey.” Health coverage can feel like navigating unfamiliar roads with confusing signs. Patriot Crest helps the consumer understand the routes, costs, and possible detours before choosing a direction. The agent is a guide—not the insurer and not a guarantee that every route is available.
## 7. Potential UMP — Unique Mechanism of the Problem
The Coverage Confusion Gap: consumers often compare plans by the most visible number—monthly premium—while the real financial and practical picture also depends on deductibles, out-of-pocket limits, coinsurance, provider networks, prescription rules, exclusions, waiting periods, underwriting, and benefit caps.
• A low premium alone does not establish that a plan is comprehensive or a good fit.
• Different product categories can use similar-sounding language while providing materially different protections.
• A person may not discover an exclusion, network restriction, or underwriting limitation until they need care.
This is a useful explanatory mechanism, not a claim that all insurers or plans are misleading.
## 8. Potential UMS — Unique Mechanism of the Solution
The Coverage Clarity Review: a structured, needs-first conversation that identifies the prospect’s priorities, checks what options may be available in their location and circumstances, and explains each relevant option in plain language.
• Discover: household, budget, doctors, prescriptions, expected care, health history where relevant, and timing.
• Categorize: distinguish ACA-compliant major medical from medically underwritten, short-term, fixed-indemnity, supplemental, or other arrangements.
• Compare: review premium and cost-sharing alongside networks, exclusions, limitations, underwriting, and enrollment/effective-date rules.
• Clarify: explain what is known, what must be verified, and what the plan does not cover.
• Decide: let the consumer choose whether to proceed, ask more questions, compare other options, or take no action.
The mechanism is the service process and standard of explanation—not a proprietary insurance product or a claim that Patriot Crest has exclusive access to every carrier.
## 9. Guru
• Primary guide: a properly licensed and appointed Patriot Crest Healthcare agent, presented as an independent guide who helps consumers understand options.
• Authority should come from verifiable licensing, carrier appointments where applicable, product knowledge, transparent disclosures, and clear explanations.
• Do not invent founder credentials, years of experience, client counts, awards, carrier relationships, or personal origin stories. Add only facts that can be substantiated.

Our role is to listen, review the options that may be available, explain the trade-offs, and help you make an informed choice.”

## 11. Product
Core offer: a no-obligation consultation and health coverage options review with a licensed agent at Patriot Crest Healthcare.
• What the prospect receives: a needs discussion; an explanation of relevant coverage categories; a review of available options the agent is licensed/appointed and authorized to discuss; and a plain-language explanation of material costs, limitations, and next steps.
• Who it is for: initially, self-employed individuals and small-business owners in Florida who need individual/family coverage or want to review their current arrangement. A separate family-focused campaign can broaden the audience.
• What it is not: not itself an insurance policy, not a guarantee of eligibility or enrollment, not a promise of savings, and not a substitute for reading official plan documents.
• Commercial details to finalize: whether the review is free; appointment format (phone/video/in person); service area; carrier/product appointments; states served; lead response time; and whether the agent can assist with Marketplace enrollment or only specified private-market products.
• Potential tagline: “Fighting high insurance costs, one family at a time.”

## 14. Belief Chains — What the Prospect Has to Believe in Order to Buy
• My current situation deserves a careful review; I should not choose based on premium alone.
• There may be more than one legitimate coverage route, but not every route provides the same protections.
• A licensed agent can help me understand options without making the decision for me.
• Patriot Crest will ask about my needs before recommending or presenting options.
• The agent will explain product type, underwriting, exclusions, networks, cost-sharing, and limitations in understandable language.
• I can ask questions, compare the information with other sources, and decline without pressure.
• Any option presented will be checked for my state, eligibility, availability, effective date, and relevant plan documents.
• I understand that the lowest premium may not be the lowest total-cost or best-fit choice.
• I have enough information to make an informed decision—or to decide not to change anything.

## 18. Other Notes
• Audience priority: Independent Ivan—the working avatar for a self-employed Floridian, roughly 35–55, with variable income and no employer contribution. This is a strategic archetype, not a statistical description of all prospects.
• Secondary audience: families who want help understanding household coverage choices. Build a separate family avatar and campaign rather than assuming one message fits every segment.
• Brand: light patriotic identity using white, navy, flag red, and soft blue with an eagle-and-shield emblem. Voice should be trustworthy, professional, approachable, family-oriented, and solution-focused.
• Approved tagline: “Fighting high insurance costs, one family at a time.” Use it as a brand line, not as a guarantee that every customer will pay less.
• Compliance guardrail: clearly distinguish ACA-compliant major medical, off-exchange ACA-compliant plans, medically underwritten products, short-term limited-duration insurance, fixed-indemnity, supplemental products, and health-sharing arrangements.
• Never state or imply that all private plans are ACA-compliant, comprehensive major medical, equivalent to Marketplace coverage, guaranteed issue, or guaranteed to cover pre-existing conditions.
• Never promise guaranteed savings, acceptance, specific benefits, doctor access, prescription coverage, or claim payment unless the exact product documents and applicable rules support the statement.
• Verify state/county availability, carrier appointments, underwriting, exclusions, provider networks, drug formularies, rates, enrollment windows, effective dates, and agent compensation before quoting or advertising.
• Use current official carrier documents, CMS/HealthCare.gov, NAIC, and Florida Office of Insurance Regulation resources for compliance and product verification.
• Research-derived consumer quotes in the earlier avatar work are public anecdotes, not Patriot Crest testimonials or proof of market-wide prevalence. Do not publish without checking original context, attribution rights, and source accuracy.
• Before launch, confirm the exact offer mechanics: free vs. paid consultation, service states, contact channels, lead handling, privacy/data retention, consent language, appointment calendar, and follow-up process.
